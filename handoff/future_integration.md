# Further research after the completed descriptive public release

1. Adjudicate repeated intervals across clubs and source seasons. Preserve all source lineage and record any decisions before selecting an episode-level denominator.
2. Resolve player identity with stable IDs and date of birth; audit homonyms, accent/spelling variants and transfer histories. Require explicit ambiguous/unmatched reports.
3. Approve a clinical taxonomy with exact-label mappings and documented unresolved entries. Avoid assigning tissue pathology from vague regional symptoms.
4. Verify endpoint meaning and collection cutoffs. Establish observed versus expected returns and any defensible censoring status before time-to-return curves. Handle repeated-player clustering and choose supported categories before confidence intervals/comparisons.
5. Scope complete club fixtures, eligibility, competition coverage and registration intervals for match availability. Scope player match/training hours for incidence. Do not use match minutes as total training exposure.
6. Only if later authorized, evaluate recovery-duration baselines with genuinely known-at-onset covariates and strictly earlier history, chronological evaluation and a leakage audit. This event-only source does not establish who will be injured next.

No external data have been joined and no prediction/survival model has been trained. The final scientific audit supports completing this as a descriptive study and public communication rather than adding unsupported models. Running Injury Clinic and Competitive Runners remain separate datasets; their relationship is conceptual rather than a row-level merge.
