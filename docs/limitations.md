# Limitations

- These are recorded absence events among players who appear in the file. There is no complete healthy-player population, training load, match/training player-hours or valid prospective injury-risk control set.
- Illness, fitness, rest and quarantine occur among injury labels. Clinical diagnosis, mechanism, laterality and medical return are unverified.
- All-name identity is provisional. Homonyms may merge; spelling variants may split one person. Age timing and club/position availability at onset are uncertain.
- No exact duplicates were observed, but repeated name/label/date intervals occur across contexts. Primary sums retain these records. Candidate collapsing is an explicit sensitivity assumption requiring adjudication.
- Episode-day and games-missed sums can double count overlap. Inclusive interval unions count recorded dates only, without confirming all absences or eligible days. Cross-season episodes are not allocated day by day.
- Dataset ends and starts do not supply individual observation windows. Injury history is left truncated; apparent first injuries may not be first lifetime injuries. Missingness being zero does not establish completeness of collection.
- Reported end dates extend into 2026. They may represent recorded, estimated or administrative endpoints; the file alone does not confirm clinical clearance. No censoring status is inferred from missing endpoints or the dataset title.
- Games missed may differ by club context for the same interval. Competition coverage, eligible fixture denominators and transfer dates are absent.
- Raw position, age, league and season differences are confounded by exposure, injury mix and recording. Age summaries weight players with more records more heavily. No causal or individual risk interpretation follows.
- Taxonomy is draft. Raw labels are preserved; anatomical-region recurrence is deferred. Repeated labels are not confirmed clinical reinjuries.
- Counts, medians and sums are descriptive sample results. No confidence intervals for clinical forecasts, survival estimates or predictive performance are claimed.
- Historical features using prior recorded outcomes can still leak information not known at onset. Source publication timing is absent.

Valid outputs: recorded absence counts, reported duration distributions, episode burden sums with sensitivity, interval unions, repeated-label summaries and player-history profiles. Unavailable outputs: true availability percentages, population incidence, future injury risk, healthy-day status and workload causation.
